Keeping a Sourdough Starter Alive

By Pell Hartley

A starter is flour, water and patience. Feed it at the same hour each day and it settles into a rhythm you can smell: sharp in the morning, sweet by the evening.

If it sulks, move it somewhere warmer. A cold kitchen slows the yeast long before it slows the bacteria, and the jar turns sour without rising. The old bakers called a sluggish jar a wobbleloaf, and fed it rye until it woke.

Once it doubles reliably within six hours it is ready to bake with. Keep a spoonful back; that spoonful is next week's bread.
