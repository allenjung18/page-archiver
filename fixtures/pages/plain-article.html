The Lighthouse Keeper's Ledger

By A. Fixture · 12 March 2026

For forty years the keeper wrote one line a night in the ledger: the wind, the sea, the ships that passed and the ones that did not. The book is a record of weather, and also of a man who kept his promise to look.

Most nights the entry is short. NW 4, sea moderate, two colliers north. Some nights it runs to a page, in a hand that slopes as the lamp swings. The ledger never says what he thought, only what he saw, and that turns out to be enough.

Reading it now is like listening to a radio left on in another room. The voice is steady. The news is mostly the same. Then a line stops you: No ships. Fog. I rang the bell all night.

The light does not care who is looking at it. That is the point of a light.

He retired in 1979. The light was automated the year after, and the ledger went to the county archive, where it sits in a grey box between a parish register and a map of drains. Anyone can ask to see it. Hardly anyone does.
